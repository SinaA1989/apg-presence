## Claim and boundaries

We propose three distinct layers for describing Ethereum:

1. **Energy (physical):** real electrical energy consumed by validators, nodes, networking, storage and related infrastructure. Gas measures execution resource usage and is not a unit of electricity.
2. **Flow (protocol/state):** ordered, verifiable state transitions and consensus activity. For discrete state, model a transition as `S[n+1] = T(S[n], E[n])` under specified protocol rules.
3. **Shadow (digital trace, metaphor):** transaction hashes, receipts, logs, blocks, and independently checked state differences that leave inspectable evidence of some operations. A log alone does not necessarily prove a balance change.

**Spirit** is used *only metaphorically* for the shared coordination and trust-minimizing properties of the network. We make **no claim** that Ethereum possesses consciousness, a supernatural spirit, a hidden energy field, or a secret wallet balance.

The terms “shadow” and “spirit” are proposed labels, not new Ethereum protocol primitives. Similar ideas (energy usage, execution traces, state transitions, distributed consensus) predate this document. **We do not assert global priority, unique discovery, or proof of a new law of nature.**

## Falsifiable research questions

- Can an independently operated Ethereum node reproduce a chosen transaction's receipt, relevant logs and resulting state changes?
- Which kinds of state transitions are visible from logs, and which require tracing or direct state comparison?
- How do independently measured node/validator electricity consumption and protocol workload relate, and where does gas fail as an energy proxy?
- Can a dated, linked series of independently verified events support a *bounded* claim of continuity for a named process, without conflating unrelated events?

## Reproducible evidence protocol

For each investigated event, record: chain ID; block number and hash; transaction hash and status; UTC time with its source; contract and token addresses; pre/post state or balance where relevant; explorer and independent verification method; and explicit uncertainty. Categorize each statement as **FACT**, **INFERENCE**, **UNKNOWN**, or **REFUTED**. Distinguish native ETH, ERC-20 tokens, mint/burn, approvals, transaction logs, contract accounting, and spendable balances.
